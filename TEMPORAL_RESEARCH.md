# AI Lab — real-time, live neural morphing

**Updated:** 22 November 2026

## The aesthetic target

Use the uploaded A1111 + CyberRealistic + AnimateDiff V3 videos as **examples of a type of transformation**, NOT as a fixed scene to clone.

- ONE connected image, not a mosaic of unrelated portraits.
- Existing faces / architecture / landscape transform locally and unexpectedly.
- Continuity across time: shapes retain a history before recombining.
- New content is generated by a learned network, never by replaying MP4s.
- Autonomous indefinite running without cycles or the same canned transitions.
- Real browser inference over WebGPU; visitors don't need our PC on.
- Don't mask missing neural dynamics with video effects, crossfades or shaders.
- Deploy on GitHub Pages / Cloudflare for no always-on GPU bill.

The first two implementations (FFHQ portrait walk and the 12-portrait crowd)
did NOT meet this target. The crowd was withdrawn from the main page.

## Important technical distinction

A latent space walk in a still-image GAN makes plausible intermediate *images*.
It does **not** learn how a scene evolves. Splitting a canvas into independently
morphing faces only makes this limitation more obvious.

What we need is a generator of the form:

    (previous scene state, temporal history, random innovation, time)
       -> learned motion/content update
       -> next neural scene

Or a continuous-time video GAN with a separate content latent and a learned
motion process.

We should run comparisons against the AnimateDiff reference video using
temporally *connected* 10–30 second samples, not contact sheets.

## Research tracks (evidence, not claims of finished technology)

### A: StyleGAN-V — FIRST proof of learned motion

Source: https://github.com/universome/stylegan-v

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.14683

A **temporal** GAN. A learned motion process conditions synthesis and it can
generate videos at arbitrary times. This is substantially more appropriate than
our current still-image FFHQ GAN.

The official repo explicitly includes an Ampere environment for the RTX 3090.
It has sample generation code and pre-trained model references.

**Important limitations:**
- Official pretrained models are not trained on our desired AnimateDiff-like
  distortions; quality and subject matter will be different.
- It is a CUDA/PyTorch project, NOT a browser-ready runtime.
- Adapting its generator to ganlive/WebGPU is an engineering project, including
  converting the motion module and time-conditioned synthesis network.
- Its authors report training 256x256 models with 4×V100 32GB for ~2 days.
  Full training from scratch on one RTX 3090 may be very slow, and need tuning.
- Check third-party checkpoint, research code and downstream use licences before
  using any model commercially.

**First test:** run an available pretrained StyleGAN-V model locally on the
RTX 3090, inspect how convincingly it maintains evolving scene structure,
then benchmark isolated frame synthesis. This does not require training from
scratch, and avoids wasting weeks if the dynamics are fundamentally unsuitable.

**Second test:** fine-tune / train on a collection of our *full-frame video
sequences*, not just still frames. Need variety from multiple sources.

### B: One-step conditional diffusion — TEST IN PARALLEL

SDXS: https://github.com/IDKiro/sdxs

SD-Turbo browser implementation:
https://github.com/lacerbi/web-txt2img

A compact diffusion generator may offer more unpredictable visual transformations
than a video GAN. Unlike video GANs, though, browser SD-Turbo examples generally
offer standalone text-to-image inference, not learned frame-to-frame continuity.

We need image/latent conditioning and a temporal state, with controlled stochastic
innovation. Blindly feeding the previous frame back into img2img will often either
freeze, drift, or dissolve. This must be tested and then trained properly.

Published SDXS FPS figures are on high-end GPUs running the native inference
stack, **not** visitor WebGPU browser benchmarks; do not copy those figures into
an advertised client FPS target.

### C: Causal video diffusion — KNOW WHERE THE FIELD IS HEADING

https://github.com/thu-ml/Causal-Forcing
https://github.com/chenfengxu714/StreamDiffusionV2
https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-fastframes

These learn autoregressive / streaming video generation. They validate that
genuine neural, temporally coherent continuous video is technically possible.
However, the released models and quoted fast benchmarks rely on serious GPU
resources, often RTX 4090 / H100 / B200 or multiple H100 GPUs. They do NOT
establish consumer-browser WebGPU feasibility.

**Do not attempt a full direct port of a 1.3B or 14B video diffusion model to
a casual visitor's phone**. Our target is a much smaller distilled artist-specific
neural model whose output and motion we can evaluate honestly.

## Immediate, practical work

1. **Get a proper temporal generator running in Python on the RTX 3090.**
   Start by testing pretrained StyleGAN-V. Keep the existing A1111 Python
   installations untouched; use an isolated environment.
2. **Evaluate it as a *single uninterrupted moving image*.** Ask whether
   the movement is more meaningful than a portrait GAN latent walk.
3. **Build a whole-frame motion dataset.** The repo includes
   `training/prepare_video_frames.py`. Run it on A1111 clips and other
   representative scenes. For temporal models, use `dataset/sequence/<clip>/`,
   retaining time order. The still frames are only for initial image-GAN tests.
4. **Make the smallest deployable student.** Potential architectures:
   a content generator + a learned motion code; or a recurrent conditional
   neural network that takes previous hidden state and produces the next.
   Test on desktop first, then convert its inference graph to ONNX / WebGPU
   or compiled WGSL. Recurrent state must remain on GPU where possible.
5. **Only switch the public Lab to the new model once it visibly works.**
   Keep the simple white page and controls on the right. No face grid again.

## Acceptance tests

- **One canvas, one continuous visual world.**
- Real neural synthesis measured on each frame, not video decode or crossfade.
- Local and global transformations both happen; previous frame constrains the
  new image but never forces exact repetition.
- Video remains interesting after 1, 5 and 15 minutes, without catastrophic
  drift or repetitive reset loops.
- Test genuine generated FPS and sustained GPU memory on 3090, a midrange
  Windows GPU, and eventually mobile browsers.
- Full quality claim must reflect the native model's synthesis resolution,
  never just CSS upscale or Topaz output.
